three more looks for Attic

Infrared, Night broadcast & One bit

Riso stays the house style. These three sit next to it under Settings → Look, each with a light and a dark side named its own way, the way Sweepy does it. Each one changes Attic's own screens: its type, colour, texture and the camera. None of them touches a photo. Prints keep their real colours on every theme, and that contrast is half the fun.

Photos are never tinted, filtered or covered Same screens, same places for everything One accent per theme, only for ready and the main action Auto follows the phone's light and dark

Infrared

The world as false-colour infrared film saw it: leaves turn crimson, skies go deep cyan, and bright things bloom with a soft red halation. The islands become crimson forests on cyan water. The main action is the film's cyan.

Halation, not gradientsHeadings and prints glow faintly crimson, the way infrared film bleeds light round anything bright. The glow sits around a print, never on it.
Bloom and SafelightBloom is pale pink paper with crimson ink. Safelight is the darkroom: near black, warm red glow, cyan for what's ready.
TypeBricolage Grotesque, heavy and a little soft, for titles; the house sans for everything else.

Night broadcast

Late-night TV after the programmes end: phosphor letters with a hair of red and blue fringe, scanlines, the dark corners of curved glass, and an on-screen display font. Albums become little TV sets with rabbit ears. The main action is broadcast amber.

Scanlines stay behind the photosThe glass, the lines and the fringes are on Attic's own surfaces. Every photo sits above them in its own clean frame, exactly as scanned.
Test card and Late nightTest card is the daytime grey with the colour bars under each title. Late night is black glass, green-white phosphor and amber.
TypeVT323, the look of a set's on-screen display, for titles and album names; the house sans for reading.
GlassPhosphorAmberBar red

One bit

Two colours and nothing between: black and white, with tone made from dither patterns, hard square shadows and striped title bars, like the first computers with a mouse. The one colour allowed is the studio's red pencil, for the main action. Then the photos arrive in full colour.

Everything else is greyIslands, shadows and the sea are drawn with ordered dither, a checkerboard of black and white, so the colour photos are the only colour on screen.
Ink and ChalkInk is black on white. Chalk swaps them: white on black, like a slate. The red pencil stays red on both.
TypePixelify Sans for titles, square and crisp; the house sans, unsmoothed, for reading.

Choosing one

Settings → Look gets the theme above the light and dark choice. The names of the two sides change with the theme, and Auto follows the phone. It changes as you tap, with no restart.

Each theme comes with its own island drawings and its own shutter. Album pages, prints and every photo stay where they are.

Album materials and the page curl

Materials are about what an album is made of, separate from the app's theme: a theme is the room, a material is the album you pick up in it. Any album can be any material. Three to start with, sketched here:

Shoebox

A loose pile instead of pages. Push prints around with your finger, pick one up, flip it to read the back. For the box of photos nobody ever stuck in.

Slide tray

A round projector tray of slides in card mounts. Swipe and it clicks round to the next, the photo thrown big on the wall.

Leather-bound

A heavy cover with the title stamped into it, black pages, prints held by little paper corners. The pages turn with a real curl.
