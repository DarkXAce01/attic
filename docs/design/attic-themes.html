Islands: same kinds, each theme's own drawing

My call: both.

The kind you pick for an album (a tent, a mountain, a lighthouse) stays with the album whatever the theme. Each theme then draws it in its own way: spot inks in Riso, a crimson forest under halation in Infrared, glowing vector lines like an old arcade screen in Night broadcast. Switch themes and your albums keep their character but change their clothes.

Why the preview's islands looked betterIn the app the drawing is the hero: a big tent or kite fills the screen and the album's photo is a little flag, or gets cut into a shape. In the preview the photo is a real print standing on a small, complete island with water, land and a few details round it.
What changesEvery island becomes a small world: water and its shadow, the land, the kind's scenery behind or beside, and the cover photo standing in front as a print, whole, never cut. Three fit on a screen instead of one and a half.
What staysYour 13 kinds, the inks you chose, the extras (birds, stars, fireflies) and the title styles. Albums you've already dressed keep their choices.

Infrared

False-colour infrared film: leaves crimson, skies cyan, bright things blooming with a red halo. Bloom is the light side, Safelight the dark. Cyan is the main action.

Halation round things, never on themTitles and prints glow a little, the way the film bled light round anything bright. Photos are never tinted.
TypeBricolage Grotesque, heavy and soft, for titles. The house sans for reading.
CameraThe frame follows the side: pale pink by day, darkroom black at night. The corners and shutter glow cyan when a print is held steady.

Night broadcast

Late-night TV: phosphor letters with a hair of red and blue fringe, scanlines, curved-glass corners, an on-screen display font. Test card is the light side, Late night the dark. Amber is the main action.

Scanlines stay behind the photosLines, glass and fringes are on Attic's own surfaces. Each photo sits above them in a rounded screen frame, clean.
TypeVT323 for titles and names, the house sans for reading. Long text is never set in the screen font.
IslandsDrawn as glowing vector lines, like an old arcade or radar screen, with the cover photo standing on each as a lit picture.

Two other directions worth a look

I looked at where photo and print design is heading. The big theme is real process and texture over polish: risograph, halftone, grain, light leaks, archival prints. Two ideas come straight out of the history of family photos, and might suit Attic even better than TV and infrared. Shown on four screens each so you can compare.

Cyanotype

The sun print: Prussian blue paper, white lines where the light got through, the way botanists and architects copied things for a century. Calm, archival, very "family archive". Sun print is the light side, Prussian the dark.

Kodachrome

The slide box and the lightbox: warm whites, the yellow and red of the film box, every photo in a rounded card slide mount, and the projector's dark room at night. Lightbox is the light side, Projector the dark.

after the themes

Album materials and the page curl

A theme is the room; a material is the album you pick up in it. Any album can be any material. Three to start with:

Shoebox

A loose pile instead of pages. Push prints around, pick one up, flip it to read the back.

Slide tray

A round projector tray. Swipe and it clicks round, the slide thrown big on the wall.

Leather-bound

Stamped cover, black pages, prints held by paper corners, and pages that turn with a real curl.
